\begin{abstract}
This is a negative/partial research report, not a Doom completeness proof.
Core Lean checks an abstract finite-capacity obstruction to lossless encoding,
an obstruction to exact fixed-clock embedding of a colliding transition into
an injective host, and a finite Rule110 truth table and trace. No universal
Turing machine (UTM), native WAD computation, or formal C refinement is
constructed. The finite-state obstruction has a specified direction and does
not refute an unrestricted existential universality claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and transition evidence}
We inspected Chocolate Doom's \texttt{src/doom/g\_game.c} and
\texttt{src/doom/p\_tick.c} at reference commit
\begin{center}\texttt{895f581c5d91497bdda0516612da803fe5843e28}.\end{center}
The two files were clean relative to that checkout. In \texttt{g\_game.c},
lines 910--1081, \texttt{G\_Ticker} handles reborns and pending game actions,
consumes player commands (with demo playback/recording branches), handles
special buttons, and dispatches by game state. In \texttt{p\_tick.c}, lines
125--153, \texttt{P\_Ticker} can return on pause/menu conditions; otherwise
it updates players, thinkers, specials and respawns, then increments level time.
Lines 46--117 show ordered linked-list thinkers and lazy removal. These are
source observations, not synchronous cellular-automaton semantics.
Pinned links and fuller caveats are in \texttt{docs/doom\_transition.md}.

A proposed boundary semantics would expose state, commands/environment inputs,
and completion, termination or fault outcomes. A total autonomous transition
requires a deterministic closure of the environment and explicit handling of
partial behavior. A sufficient state must account for globals/statics, heap and
ordered thinkers, RNG state, timing, commands and relevant I/O, not merely a
screen image. No complete record or C semantics is formalized. In particular,
allocation and growable demo buffers cannot be assumed bounded from these two
files alone. No numerical bound on Doom's state space is proved.

\section{Checked abstract obstructions}
Let $e:A\to B$ encode guest states into host states. If a decoder satisfies
$d(e(x))=x$ for all $x$, then $e$ is injective. Thus a witnessed collision
$x\ne y$, $e(x)=e(y)$ excludes such a decoder.

For a duplicate-free list $L$ of source states and a finite list $H$ covering
all values of $e$, injectivity implies $|L|\leq |H|$. Consequently
$|H|<|L|$ excludes injection and yields a collision. A source admitting distinct
lists longer than every natural bound cannot be losslessly encoded into one
fixed finite host cover. These statements, including their explicit cover and
unbounded-sample hypotheses, are checked in
\texttt{proofs/DoomComplete.lean}. They are not applications to an enumerated
Doom state space.

Let $g:A\to A$, $h:B\to B$, let $e$ be injective, and fix $k\in\mathbb N$.
If $h$ is injective and
\[
 h^k(e(x))=e(g(x))\qquad\text{for all }x,
\]
then $g$ is injective: equality of guest successors gives equality of encoded
successors; injectivity of $h^k$ and $e$ recovers equality of predecessors.
A witnessed collision of $g$ therefore excludes this exact fixed-clock
embedding. This also covers $k=0$ and needs no finiteness assumption.
Reversibility is used only through injectivity. Neither injectivity nor a
full-state collision of the actual Doom transition is proved here.

\section{Why these are not global refutations}
The finite-capacity result obstructs lossless encoding of arbitrarily many
guest configurations \emph{into} a fixed finite closed host. It does not
obstruct encoding a finite Doom system into an unbounded universal machine.
The reversible obstruction applies only with its extra injectivity and exact
fixed-clock hypotheses, not to every possible universal machine.
A projected simulation $d(h^k(e(x)))=g(x)$ can retain history in the host and
is not the same equation. Restricted states, variable clocks, resource-indexed
families and general input/output universality are outside these negative
conclusions. No unrestricted impossibility result is asserted.

\section{Bounded Rule110 fallback and Blocked Workstreams}
In \texttt{substrate/utm\_proof.lean}, the local rule is
$(c\mathbin{\&}\neg l)\mathbin{|}(c\ne r)$ on Booleans. In neighborhood order
$111,110,101,100,011,010,001,000$, its outputs are
$0,1,1,0,1,1,1,0$. Lean checks agreement with eight explicit pattern cases.
The executable row update uses a fixed-length list and false exterior neighbors
reset at each step (not a ring or an infinite-domain theorem). Width is
preserved and a trace of $n$ updates has $n+1$ rows. Kernel-checked reduction gives
\[
0001000\ \to\ 0011000\ \to\ 0111000\ \to\ 1101000\ \to\ 1111000.
\]
This is four updates at width seven, not a universality proof. A separate
conditional lemma merely composes two assumed one-step commuting diagrams;
it supplies no implementation or encoding witnesses.

\section{Literature and blocked construction}
Cook proves universality of Rule110 using a global construction with cyclic
tag systems and organized backgrounds/signals~\cite{cook}. Its background
conditions cannot be replaced without proof by our finite zero-exterior row.
Neary and Woods prove P-completeness of predicting $t$ steps of Rule110 via
polynomial-time deterministic Turing-machine simulation~\cite{nearywoods}.
Neither result supplies a Doom gadget implementation; neither is formalized
here. Local truth-table agreement does not establish the global reduction.

\texttt{substrate/utm\_spec.md} explicitly marks the UTM construction blocked.
Missing witnesses include a concrete WAD substrate, a uniform effective input
compiler, initialization and invariant proofs, scheduling and memory mechanisms,
resource semantics, clock and halt/output decoding, and refinement from C or
binary execution. An externally computed command/demo trace is not evidence
of native WAD computation. Specifically, we document five major blocked workstreams:
(A) Native In-Engine Rule 110 Gadget (creating a purely Vanilla-compatible map/lump gadget driven by the engine ticker),
(B) Formal C Semantics (mechanizing a sufficient subset of Chocolate Doom's C execution in Lean),
(C) Python/WAD Correspondence Mechanization (proving the Python compiler output matches the WAD spec mathematically),
(D) Strong Existential Proof (exhibiting a Universal Machine and proving the embedding without a trivial pullback tautology), and
(F) Scale and Performance Limits (without a native gadget, true engine limits cannot be evaluated beyond what is accepted by the visualization compiler).
No formal C semantics proof is claimed, and the creation of a purely native
gadget is abandoned as computationally infeasible within the present scope.

\section{Executable fallback and empirical evidence}
The shipped \texttt{doomc} accepts periodic Rule110 JSON specifications and
emits a static trace gallery as a vanilla E1M1 PWAD. This boundary convention
is different from the formal zero-exterior trace above; no refinement between
these two implementations is claimed. For width eight and seven updates,
the map has 81 sectors, including entrance corridors. Bright cells encode one
and dark cells zero. An exit linedef permits ordinary interactive exploration.
The requested filename \texttt{doom\_utm.wad} is retained, but the artifact is
not a UTM. Python computes every cell before Doom starts.

A generated vanilla version-109 demo contains 172 tic commands. Two actual
Chocolate Doom 3.1.1 headless playbacks, using Freedoom 0.13.0, write savegames
at level time 170. The verifier checks the tic count and all sectors' floor,
ceiling, light, special and tag values, then compares a SHA256 projection hash.
The two projections match. This is not a full game-state hash, a rendering
check, a recorded human playthrough, or evidence of in-engine CA evaluation.
Commands and logs accompany the artifacts. Compiler tests cover sizes
$N=1,2,4,8,16,32$; $N=64$ is rejected by explicit backend policy, not by an
impossibility theorem or measured engine limit. Tics per simulated CA step
are undefined because the engine executes no CA update.

\section{Executable PDF: documented limitation}
The executable-PDF stretch goal was not implemented. Our deliverable is a
static typeset report, not an impossibility theorem about PDF. JavaScript APIs,
security policies and rendering facilities vary by viewer. A native executable
cannot simply be assumed to run inside a document, but this does not exclude a
JavaScript port, emulator or viewer-specific implementation. No such port or
viewer benchmark was attempted here. We provide the PWAD and demo separately.
The manuscript can be rebuilt with two invocations of
\texttt{pdflatex -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error doom\_complete.tex}
from the paper directory. Local validation used MiKTeX 25.12.

\section*{Anticipated Reviewer 2}
\emph{``Weak completeness is trivial.''} Correct, and labelled as such: it
appears only as a conditional composition lemma with every premise explicit.
\emph{``Where is the universal machine?''} Absent, deliberately: the UTM
section enumerates the missing witnesses rather than gesturing at them.
\emph{``Your obstruction does not touch the existential claim.''} Also correct,
and stated in the theorem's own direction: it bounds unbounded guests inside a
fixed finite closed installation, not Doom inside an unbounded substrate.
\emph{``The gallery is not computation.''} It is not offered as computation;
each artifact that mentions it says precomputed. \emph{``Prove it end to end in
Lean.''} That requires a mechanized C semantics for the engine, which nobody
involved has, and which we decline to fake.

\section{Conclusion}
The delivered fallback is an abstract obstruction library and a bounded
Rule110 calculation, not Doom completeness. A native WAD UTM and the semantic
bridge to engine execution remain unconstructed. The negative results retain
their hypotheses and directions; the broader existential question remains open
in this project.
